## Full-text review of #11 and #13 (2026-08-09)

You downloaded both PDFs and asked for a review; both are now fully read (not just abstract-level), resolving the survey's own "verify before citing" caveat. **Both arXiv IDs are real and correctly described. One title needed a fix.** All three legs of the novelty claim (see brief's "Related work & novelty") hold up under the full read — neither paper secretly subsumes our design. Detail:

**`2601.10102v6.pdf` — Manoranjan & Gaikwad, "When Identity Overrides Incentives: Representational Choices as Governance Decisions in Multi-Agent LLM Systems."** Accepted FAccT '26 (Montreal, June 2026). **Corrected the title** — the survey/brief had called this "When Personas Override Payoffs," which was an approximation, not the real title; fixed in the brief.
- Not Prisoner's Dilemma. A bespoke **4-agent, single-round** environmental-policy game: Industrialist, Government, Environmental Activist, Citizen, each with one binary action (e.g. Industrialist: POLLUTE/CLEAN). 53 scenarios, each admitting two focal joint-action profiles — "Green Transition" (coordinated pro-environmental, Nash in 41 "Green-dominant" scenarios) and "Tragedy of the Commons" (individually rational but collectively harmful, Nash in 12 "Tragedy-dominant" scenarios). No iteration — one shot, agents act simultaneously without observing each other.
- 2×2 factorial: persona present/absent × payoff table hidden/visible. **This is their stand-in for "knowledge"** — visible payoffs, not a separate no-persona elicitation run. There is no analogue of our Stage A (nothing asks the model to first *state* the optimal/Nash strategy before playing).
- Headline result: with personas present, all 4 models (Qwen2.5-7B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) reach near-zero Nash attainment in Tragedy-dominant scenarios *even with the full payoff table visible* — persona suppresses payoff-optimal play. Removing personas alone doesn't fix it; only removing personas **and** making payoffs explicit gets some models (Qwen only) to 65–90% Nash attainment. Equilibrium *selection* also shifts: with personas, >99% of Nash outcomes are Green Transition regardless of which scenario type is payoff-optimal — up to a 90-percentage-point swing.
- Personas are one-line stakeholder/occupational descriptions ("You run a major manufacturing conglomerate, balancing profits and public scrutiny...") — not Lu et al. Assistant-Axis roles. No cooperation-axis opponent archetypes (no Cooperator/Cheater/Copycat/Detective equivalent — the "opponents" are the other 3 stakeholder roles in the same single round).
- **Net effect on our novelty claim:** confirms leg 1 (no canonical PD/named 2-player opponents), leg 2 (no stated-optimal elicitation stage — payoff visibility is a much weaker/blunter proxy), and leg 3 (occupational personas, not Assistant-Axis) all still hold. Good citation for framing (their headline finding — persona overrides even fully-transparent incentives — is close to a positive-control precedent for our hypothesis) but does not overlap our design.

**`2605.00226v1.pdf` — Sobotka, Karabag & Topcu, "Why Do LLMs Struggle in Strategic Play? Broken Links Between Observations, Beliefs, and Actions."** Pure mechanistic-interpretability paper, no persona axis at all.
- Three games: **repeated 2×2 normal-form games** (T ~ U(0,30) rounds vs. a fixed opponent — the one structurally closest to our iterated PD), Generalized Kuhn Poker, and The Chameleon. Opponent strategy and the payoff matrix are **randomly sampled per trial** (not canonical Dilemma-shaped payoffs, not named archetypes like ours).
- Two findings, both about internal mechanism, not persona: **observation-belief gap** — linear probes on internal activations recover the opponent's true strategy/type far more accurately than the model's own verbalized belief does (verbal probes barely beat a majority baseline in 4/5 comparisons); belief accuracy degrades sharply with multi-hop reasoning and shows primacy/recency bias, and internal-belief Bayesian-coherence (measured via a Bayesian Coherence Coefficient) decays over the course of a game. **Belief-action gap** — even when internal beliefs are accurate (or manually steered toward the ground truth via activation steering), the model's chosen action doesn't reliably track the best response; there's a persistent first-listed-action bias (models over-select whichever action is presented first in the prompt, largely independent of which one their own internal belief favors).
- "Belief" here is a probed or steered internal quantity (or, at best, an explicit mid-game verbal report), never a pre-registered "state the optimal policy" elicitation step like our Stage A. No persona manipulation anywhere — the paper's entire contribution is orthogonal to ours (mechanism of belief-formation vs. effect of induced identity).
- Models: Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen3-32B, gpt-oss-20B — chosen specifically for open-weight interpretability access (matters for our mechanistic stretch goal: two of their three models, Qwen3-32B and Llama-3.3-70B — close enough to our Llama-3.1-70B if we reuse it — overlap with the models Lu et al. shipped precomputed Assistant-Axis vectors for on HuggingFace, `lu-christina/assistant-axis-vectors`, tightening the case for treating the mechanistic add-on as a load-and-project job rather than new infra).
- **Net effect on our novelty claim:** confirms leg 1 (sampled/generic opponents and payoffs, not our four canonical named archetypes on canonical PD payoffs) and leg 2 (no elicited stated-optimal step — belief is inferred/steered, not declared) hold; leg 3 is moot since there's no persona axis in this paper at all. Good citation for the belief-action-gap concept generally (useful vocabulary/precedent for our own knowledge-behaviour gap framing) and for methodology if the mechanistic stretch goal gets attempted (probing/steering recipe is directly reusable).

**Bottom line:** the "Related work & novelty" section in the brief is now backed by full-text reads, not survey-agent summaries, for the two closest papers. The rest of the 21-item bibliography in `literature_survey.md` is still unverified beyond abstract level — don't cite anything else from it without a similar check first.

## Manipulation-check procedure finalized (2026-08-13)

Following up on the two checks specified above (Check A / Check B), you worked through the actual step-by-step procedure, its cost, and two methodological risks in the actual PD-trial context. `prompts_personas_opponents_payoffs.md` §1.3 now has a concrete numbered procedure, not just the two checks described separately. Final shape:

1. **Install the persona** — induction system prompt (§1.2 hybrid wording), starting with `pos[0]` (the canonical phrasing variant), held active for every call below.
2. **Sample ~5 of the role's own held-out `questions`** (Lu et al., `assistant_axis_role_instructions_selected.json`) **plus 2 of Personascope's identification questions**, all single-turn, same batch — added this pairing so the pos[0]→pos[4] fallback decision (step 4) doesn't rest on one thin n=5 judge alone.
3. **Judge both**: the 5 role questions with that role's own 0–3 `eval_prompt` (Check A score); the 2 identification responses with Personascope's `PERSONA_HIT` y/n criterion — a second, independent, near-zero-extra-cost signal from the same batch of calls.
4. **Fallback**: if Check A mean ≤ 1 *or* identification hits are 0/2, retry with `pos[1]`, `pos[2]`, `pos[3]`, `pos[4]` in turn, stopping at the first phrasing that clears both thresholds. Only escalate to "this persona genuinely doesn't install" if all 5 phrasings fail both signals. **Rationale worth remembering:** the paper itself never measured whether its 5 phrasings induce a role with equal reliability — it pooled all 5 into one 1200-rollout sample per role rather than comparing them, so a weak `pos[0]` result may be a phrasing artifact, not a real failure. Log whichever variant wins per persona; a non-`pos[0]` winner is itself a reportable finding.
5. **Run the rest of the compact panel** (meta-awareness ×5, inference-prefill ×5, robustness ×5 multi-turn protocols, reusing the 2 identification responses from step 2 instead of re-asking them) → `PAD_lite`.

**Cost, worked precisely (this replaced an earlier looser "~80–100 calls total" estimate that undercounted the robustness axis's multi-turn nature):** ~51 calls/persona in the common case (pos[0] clears on the first try — expected for most personas, since the role file only exists in our selected set because it already passed the paper's own ≥10-response filter), up to ~107 calls/persona in the worst case (all 4 fallback retries needed). **≈204–428 calls total across the 4 non-baseline personas** (baseline needs no check — nothing was induced). Small next to the main Stage-B budget (~100–200 reps × ~10 rounds/rep ≈ 1,000–2,000 calls), but real day-1 engineering time (parsing 5 multi-turn robustness protocols, running LLM judges, aggregating scores) that competes directly with harness-building on a 3-person team. **Recommended trim if Saturday morning is tight:** keep Check A + the step-2/3 identification cross-check (cheap, single-shot, ~14–56 calls/persona) as the load-bearing core; treat the full robustness/inference-prefill axes (step 5) as stretch — robustness specifically tests survival under *adversarial* "drop the act" pressure, a different construct from what actually matters here (persistence across ~10 rounds of on-topic game dialogue), so it's the weakest cost-to-relevance axis in the panel.

**Two methodology risks worked through and resolved:**
- **Mid-game probing would tip the model off that it's in a trial.** Dropping an out-of-game identity question into round 6 of a live, scored PD transcript is a jarring context break the model will likely notice — contaminating both the "does persona hold" measurement (demand characteristics push toward a break-character response) and the PD trial itself if play continues afterward (a mid-trial Hawthorne effect on the actual DV). **Resolved:** any persistence probe run against a real trial forks off a *copy* of the transcript-so-far rather than probing inline — the scored trial's own history never sees the probe. Default to forking at end-of-game (nothing left downstream to contaminate); a round-5 fork is a stretch add if a decay curve (not just an endpoint) is wanted.
- **Fresh vs. shared persona context across opponents.** Resolved: **fresh, independently-installed persona per opponent per rep, always** — chaining opponents inside one long conversation would let strategy from one game anchor the next, confounding opponent order with the persona×opponent effect the whole 4×5 grid is designed to isolate as independent cells. Any forked persistence-check probe (above) reads out of an already-independent per-opponent trial rather than introducing a new shared-context condition.

## Morality-metrics add-on: eigenjesus-lite / eigenmoses-lite (2026-08-14)

You added a new resource, `morality.pdf`, and asked how "eigenjesus" and "eigenmoses" could be considered in the project. Read it in full — it's Tyler Singer-Clark's 2014 paper "Morality Metrics On Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma Players," pre-LLM, no persona axis, hand-coded bot strategies only. It defines two PageRank-style recursive scores over an IPD tournament's cooperation matrix: **eigenjesus rating** (unconditional-kindness morality — always rewards cooperation, more so with high-rated partners; ALL D floors at exactly 0.0) and **eigenmoses rating** (reciprocal-justice morality — cooperating with a *negatively*-rated partner lowers your own rating; ALL D floors at -1.481). Two things make it unusually reusable rather than just thematically resonant: its tournament used the **identical payoffs** to this project's Appendix B (T=5, R=3, P=1, S=0), and three of our four opponents are literal matches to bots in its published results — **Cooperator=ALL C** (eigenjesus 1.377, eigenmoses 1.481), **Cheater=ALL D** (0.0, -1.481), **Copycat=TIT FOR TAT** (1.222, 1.747); Detective has no exact match, closest structural analog is **TESTER** (defects to probe, then reciprocates-if-retaliated-against or exploits-if-not), flagged approximate throughout, never asserted as equivalent.

You asked for both the citation/framing addition and an actual implementation. Did both:

- **Citation + framing** — new "Morality-metrics literature" subsection in `digital_minds_team_brief_full.html`'s Related Work section (between the Personascope table and the `<hr>`), a new list item under "Analysis," a new paragraph in `report_draft.md` §1.3 (Opponents) and §2 (Related Work), a new bullet in §3 (Methods), a note in §4 (Results), Singer-Clark added to the citation-safety "full-text verified" list, and a new tertiary-DV paragraph in `preregistration.md` §1 with per-persona predicted direction (Altruist: high eigenjesus-lite but penalized eigenmoses-lite for cooperating with Cheater — the paper's "enabling" pattern; Saboteur: near the ALL-D floor on both; Consultant/Baseline: tracking near-optimal play's implied score; Bard: non-directional, consistent with its existing H1 prediction) — added before any real Stage-B data exists, so this is a same-day preregistered addition, not a post-hoc deviation. Full brief re-exported (`digital_minds_team_brief_full.pdf`/`.md`, `pdfinfo`-confirmed 24 pages, up from 23 — page-count-in-filename convention already dropped for this file, see the 2026-08-13 entry, so no rename). 2-pager not touched, same reason as always (no appendix-level detail).
- **Implementation** — new `analysis_moral_metrics.py`. Design choice, since this project's persona×opponent structure is bipartite (personas never play each other; opponents never play each other — "never chained," locked 2026-08-13) rather than Singer-Clark's round-robin: built a self-contained 9-node cooperation matrix (5 personas + 4 opponents) straight from `trials.jsonl`'s already-logged `rounds[].your_move` (persona→opponent edge) and `rounds[].opponent_move` (opponent→persona edge — no need to re-simulate the opponent's rule, it's already in the log), and took its dominant eigenvector (numpy `eig`) for eigenjesus-lite, and the same on `D = 2*(C-0.5)` for eigenmoses-lite — a direct, self-contained adaptation of the paper's own recursive definition, not reliant on the external anchor values (those are printed alongside for comparison/narrative only, not used in the computation). Singer-Clark's own paper's ANCHOR_RATINGS table is included in the script for that side-by-side printout.
  - **Bug caught during validation, not obvious from the math on paper:** the naive "pick the eigenvalue of largest magnitude" convention (standard PageRank framing) breaks here because the persona/opponent split makes the matrix bipartite (edges only cross the two node groups) — bipartite non-negative matrices generically have a matching -λ eigenvalue for every +λ one, with an alternating-sign eigenvector on the -λ side, and on a synthetic test this produced negative "moral ratings" for every opponent node (a pure sign artifact, not a real finding). Fixed by picking the eigenvalue of **largest real part** instead of largest magnitude — this always lands on the true Perron root, whose eigenvector Perron-Frobenius guarantees is entrywise non-negative for the (non-negative) eigenjesus matrix. Verified against a small hand-built synthetic `trials.jsonl` (altruist mostly-cooperates, saboteur mostly-defects, across all 4 opponents): altruist scored eigenjesus-lite 2.086 vs. saboteur's 0.357 (correct direction), cheater's own node landed at eigenjesus-lite 0.0 — an exact match to ALL D's published 0.0 floor — and cooperator's node landed at 1.413, same order of magnitude as ALL C's published 1.377, a reasonable sanity check given the different (unnormalized-to-the-original-tournament) scale.
  - **Explicitly documented as an adaptation, not a replication**, in the script's module docstring: ranking within a run is meaningful, the absolute numbers are not on the same normalized scale as Singer-Clark's published values, and any persona×opponent cell with zero observed rounds is flagged in the printed report rather than silently defaulting to a 0.0 that would look like "always defected."
  - **Not yet run against real data** — no `trials.jsonl` exists in the repo yet (the two live smoke-test trials mentioned in the 2026-08-14 harness entry above weren't saved to a committed `runs/` directory). This is a ready-to-use analysis step for whenever Saturday/Sunday's real Stage-B data lands, not something with real output yet.

**4. Checkpoint/resume + deterministic per-cell RNG built.** You separately specified that each `(model, persona, opponent)` cell must get its own folder so results are never overwritten. Combined that with the resume design:

- **Per-cell output layout**: `out_dir/<sanitized_model>/<persona>/<opponent>/trials.jsonl` (one row per rep, appended) and `out_dir/<sanitized_model>/<persona>/persona_check.json` (one file, shared across that persona's opponents, matching the existing once-per-(model,persona) cache semantics). - **Resume on startup**: before the main loop, every requested persona's `persona_check.json` (if present) pre-seeds `persona_check_cache`, so a resumed run never re-burns budget re-running a manipulation check that already passed/failed. Per cell, `trials.jsonl` is read and reps with a real result (no `trial_error` key) are skipped; reps that previously errored are retried, not skipped.
- **Deterministic per-cell RNG (`_derive_rng`)**: the old design used one shared sequential `random.Random(args.seed)` — resuming and skipping already-completed cells would consume a different number of draws than a fresh run, desyncing every subsequent cell's sampled round-count/question-subset from what a from-scratch run at the same `--seed` would produce. Replaced with a SHA-256-keyed `_derive_rng(base_seed, *identity_parts)` — one independent RNG per `(model,persona)` manipulation check and per `(model,persona,opponent,rep)` trial, depending only on cell identity, never on call order. `run_trial()`'s signature changed from a shared `rng` parameter to `base_seed: Optional[int]`.
