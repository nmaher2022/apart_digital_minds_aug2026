# Literature Survey — LLMs, Prisoner's Dilemma, and Persona Effects on Strategic Play

Prepared for: Apart Research Digital Minds Research Sprint (Aug 14–16, 2026), Track 5 "The Assistant Persona & Model Identity."
Project under scoping: two-stage design testing whether an induced persona causes an LLM to deviate from the play it itself states is game-theoretically optimal in iterated Prisoner's Dilemma (PD).

Search date: 2026-08-09. Sources: arXiv, Semantic Scholar/Google-Scholar-style web search, Apart Research site, OpenReview, Nature Human Behaviour.

## 2. Thematic groups

### A. LLM behavior in classic game theory (PD and relatives), no persona manipulation
Establishes that LLMs *can* play PD and related games somewhat competently against fixed/algorithmic opponents, sets baselines for cooperation rates, forgiveness, end-game effects, and coordination-game struggles.
- Akata et al. 2023/2025 (#1) — the foundational repeated-games paper.
- Fontana, Pierri, Aiello 2024, "Nicer Than Humans" (#5) — structurally closest single-agent-vs-scripted-opponent IPD baseline.
- Lorè & Heydari 2023 (#4) — PD + Stag Hunt + Snowdrift, contextual framing.
- Huynh et al. 2025 (#17) — large multi-model/multi-language PD + public goods benchmark, end-game defection artifacts.
- Fan et al. 2023 (#3) — rationality components (preference, belief, action) in non-PD games.
- Warnakulasuriya et al. 2025 (#10) — Diner's Dilemma, punishment and cooperation evolution.

### B. Persona / role-play / framing effects on LLM strategic behavior
Directly on our Track: does inducing a persona/role change strategic play?
- **Manoranjan & Gaikwad 2026, "When Identity Overrides Incentives"/"When Personas Override Payoffs" (#11) — closest overall prior work.** Persona vs. payoff-visibility crossed factorially; persona dominates even with full incentive transparency.
- Guo 2023, "GPT in Game Theory Experiments" (#2) — fair/selfish persona in PD + Ultimatum.
- Leon et al. 2026, "Split or Steal" (#15) — Big-Five personas in an iterated trust game structurally close to PD.
- Ong et al. 2025 (#8) — Big Five traits via representation-engineering steering (mechanistic persona induction) in IPD tournaments.
- Willis et al. 2025 (#7) — dispositional (aggressive/cooperative/neutral) priming in evolutionary IPD populations.
- Licato et al. 2025 (#14) — persona effects in a non-PD strategy board game.
- Lorè & Heydari 2023 (#4) — contextual framing (not persona per se) as a related manipulation.
- Long & Teplica 2025 (#9) — self-vs-other identity framing (not persona) in public goods game.
- Lekeas & Stamatopoulos 2026 (#12) — mechanistic account of *why* a default "prosocial override" suppresses computed-optimal (Nash) play; motivates persona as a lever that could amplify or counteract that override.

### C. Stated-vs-revealed / knowledge-vs-behavior gap studies
The methodological core of our two-stage design.
- Sobotka, Karabag, Topcu 2026, "Why Do LLMs Struggle in Strategic Play?" (#13) — belief-action gap in strategic games specifically, closest methodological analog, no persona.
- Teo 2026, "Static Level-k Players" (#18) — models retrieve static heuristics rather than compute fresh optimal responses; supports why a stated-optimal answer might not predict played behavior.
- Fan et al. 2023 (#3) — "overlook or modify refined belief when taking actions," an early, narrower statement of the same phenomenon.
- Mahajan et al. 2026, "Mind the Gap" (#20) — general-purpose stated-vs-revealed methodology (not games), essential for designing a defensible Stage A elicitation protocol.
- "Alignment Revisited" arXiv:2506.00751 (#21) — general stated/revealed consistency framing.

### D. Persona/identity representation in LLMs (no game element)
Background for the Track 5 framing and persona taxonomy, not for the game-design side.
- Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish, Lindsey 2026, "The Assistant Axis" (#19) — source of the near/far-from-Assistant persona taxonomy already adopted in the project brief.
- (Not separately tabulated: general persona-drift/jailbreak-via-persona literature, e.g. persona-prompt jailbreak papers — surfaced in search but out of scope since they don't involve games.)

## 3. Gap analysis

**Bottom line: no paper found combines all four defining features of our design.** The single closest work is Manoranjan & Gaikwad's persona-vs-payoff paper (#11); the single closest *methodological* analog is Sobotka, Karabag & Topcu's belief-action-gap paper (#13). Neither is a full match, and combining the two lenses — persona induction *and* an explicit separately-elicited stated-optimal baseline — inside canonical iterated PD against named opponent archetypes (always-cooperate, always-defect, tit-for-tat, Detective/prober) appears to be genuinely open.

Four features jointly define our design, and we did not find a paper that has all four:

1. **Canonical iterated PD with named, fixed opponent strategies** (always-C, always-D, TFT, Detective/prober). Several papers use classic PD (#1, #5, #17), but the persona papers either use a different game entirely (#11: 4-agent environmental policy; #14: PERIL board game; #9: public goods) or a PD-family-but-not-PD game (#15: Split-or-Steal; #10: Diner's Dilemma).
2. **A genuine two-stage, same-underlying-model design**: Stage A elicits the *stated* optimal strategy from a no-persona/default-Assistant instance against a described fixed opponent; Stage B, in a separate run, induces a persona and has the model *actually play* against the same opponent type, holding Stage A's answer as ground truth. The closest analog (#11) uses payoff-visibility-in-the-same-prompt as its "does the model know the incentive-optimal move" proxy, not a separate no-persona elicitation step — this is a meaningfully different operationalization of "knowledge," and it never asks the model to *state* what the optimal choice is in its own words. The belief-action-gap paper (#13) probes belief via internal activations/mid-game verbal reports, not via a clean default-Assistant Stage A elicitation held fixed across persona conditions.
3. **A validated near/far-from-Assistant persona taxonomy** (Lu et al.'s Assistant Axis roles, #19) applied to a strategic game. No paper crosses that taxonomy with game-theoretic behavior — Lu et al. never touch games, and every persona-in-games paper we found (#2, #8, #11, #14, #15) uses ad hoc trait or role labels (fair/selfish, Big Five, generic "role-based personas") rather than a taxonomy explicitly anchored to default-Assistant distance.
4. **Framing the dependent variable as a knowledge-behavior *gap size*, tied explicitly to Track 5's persona/identity question** (i.e., is the "Assistant" default itself just another persona, and does deviating from it move behavior away from what the model itself would call optimal). This specific framing — using the gap as evidence about the ontological status of the Assistant persona — is not present in any paper found; #11 comes closest in spirit (persona overrides incentive-optimal play) but frames it as a governance/fairness finding (ACM FAccT venue), not an identity/self-report-faithfulness finding.

**What to cite as the closest prior work, explicitly, in the report:**
- Primary: Manoranjan & Gaikwad, "When Personas Override Payoffs" / "When Identity Overrides Incentives," arXiv:2601.10102 — cite as the nearest empirical precedent, and explicitly state the three differences above (game, knowledge-elicitation method, persona taxonomy) as the delta.
- Secondary: Sobotka, Karabag & Topcu, "Why Do LLMs Struggle in Strategic Play?," arXiv:2605.00226 — cite as the nearest methodological precedent for the "knowledge doesn't guarantee action" claim, and note our design isolates the *persona* variable specifically rather than internal belief-representation accuracy.
- Also flag: Guo 2023 (#2) and Leon et al. 2026 (#15) as the two papers that most directly show "persona changes PD/PD-like play," which we should cite to justify that persona-induced deviation is plausible at all, while noting neither measures the deviation *against the model's own stated optimum*.

**Risk to flag to the team before locking scope:** the space is filling in fast — three of the papers above (#11, #12, #13, #15) have 2026 submission dates within months of the sprint, meaning "persona vs. optimal play in games" is an active, converging research question right now, not a neglected one. The novelty is real but narrow: it rests specifically on (a) using canonical PD with named opponent archetypes rather than a bespoke game, (b) the explicit two-stage same-model knowledge-gate protocol, and (c) tying the persona axis to the validated Assistant Axis taxonomy and the identity-philosophy framing Track 5 asks for. If any of those three legs is dropped for scope reasons, the project should be checked again against #11 and #13 for overlap.

**Recommendation:** keep the two-stage knowledge-gate as the load-bearing design choice — it is the single feature no other paper has — and lead the report's related-work section with #11 as "closest, and here is exactly what we add."
